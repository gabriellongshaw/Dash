Dash guide

Dash is a free endless runner that plays in your browser. You run automatically and your job is to jump, slide and double jump through a course that gets faster the longer you survive. This guide covers the controls, every obstacle, how scoring and difficulty work, and how to spend your coins. It was written by Gabriel Longshaw, who built the game.

Controls

Dash works with a keyboard or a touch screen.

  • Jump: Space, the up arrow or W. Press again in the air to double jump.
  • Slide: hold the down arrow or S. Pressing it in mid-air makes you fall faster, which helps you land and slide sooner.
  • Pause: P or Esc.
  • Touch: tap the screen or the jump button to jump, and hold the slide button to slide.

Every run starts with a short countdown, so you have time to get ready after pressing play.

Side view and third person

Before each run you pick a view, and Dash remembers your choice on that device.

Side view (Classic) is a flat course seen from the side. Obstacles arrive from the right and you only have two decisions: jump or slide.

Third person puts the camera behind your runner on a three-lane track, in the style of games such as Subway Surfers. On a keyboard use the left and right arrows or A and D to change lane. On touch, swipe left or right to change lane, up to jump and down to slide. Each swipe does one thing, so a long drag will not change several lanes at once.

Coins, quests and the shop are shared between both views, but third person has its own best score and its own leaderboard. Score quests only count runs in Classic. Obstacles are the same in both views, but in third person they sit in lanes, so many can be dodged by moving sideways instead of jumping. Wide barriers, ceiling gates, force fields and wide spikes cover every lane and still need a jump or a slide.

Scoring and coins

Your score is built from two things: the distance you run and the coins you collect. Each coin is worth 5 points and distance adds the rest. Coins also go into your wallet when the run ends, so a good run does two jobs at once: it raises your best score and pays for items in the shop.

Coins are placed along the course in lines, arcs over obstacles and ladders up tall walls. Following an arc usually means you are already in the right position to clear the obstacle beneath it.

How the difficulty rises

The run starts at a gentle pace and speeds up quickly in the first minute or so. After that the speed keeps rising more slowly until it reaches a fixed maximum, so a long run does not become impossible just by getting faster. Instead the challenge changes in two other ways:

  • Harder obstacles unlock in tiers. The first stretch is made of crates, spikes, gates, tall walls and drones. Seven further tiers then bring in rollers and buzzsaws, then missiles and force fields, then bouncing orbs, and finally long combinations that chain several obstacles together.
  • The gaps shrink. The space between obstacles gets tighter with every tier, and keeps shrinking slowly past the last one down to a minimum, so you get less time to recover between challenges.

The game also shows a short hint the first time you meet an obstacle that needs a specific move, such as a ceiling gate (hold to slide) or a tall wall (double jump).

Obstacles and how to beat them

  • Crates and spikes: jump over them. These are the basic obstacles and you will meet them from the first second.
  • Tall walls: too high for a single jump. Double jump, and look for the coin ladder that often leads up the side of the wall.
  • Ceiling gates: slide underneath. Start sliding a little early and hold the slide until the gate has passed.
  • Drones: hover low above the ground. You can slide under them or jump over. Some of them bob up and down, so watch their rhythm before committing.
  • Rollers: fast wheels that arrive sooner than they look. Jump a touch earlier than you would for a crate.
  • Buzzsaws: blades that roll toward you along the ground and get faster the further you run. Jump over them.
  • Bouncing orbs: spiked balls that hop along the ground. Jump when the orb is low, and slip underneath when it is high.
  • Missiles: fast rockets announced by a warning triangle at the edge of the screen. Jump low ones and slide under higher ones, and use the warning to decide before the missile appears.
  • Force fields: wide barriers that need one long, well-timed jump.
  • Trains (third person only): long carriages that fill a whole lane. You cannot jump or slide them, so change lane early. Some roll toward you.

Tips for a higher score

  • Look ahead, not at your runner. Reading the next obstacle one screen early gives you time to choose between a jump and a slide.
  • Save your double jump. It is the only way over tall walls, so avoid using it for ordinary crates and you will not be caught without it.
  • Fast-fall to recover. If you jumped too early and a second obstacle is right behind the first, press down in the air to land sooner.
  • Follow the coins. Coin arcs mark a safe jump path over an obstacle. They are usually the easiest line to take.
  • Do not rush the start. The first obstacles are slow and forgiving. Use them to settle into a rhythm before the speed climbs.
  • Pick a view you can read. Classic is about judging heights and timing, while third person adds lane planning. Each has its own best score, so you can practise one without affecting the other.
  • Pause if you need to. P or Esc stops the run, and the countdown gives you a moment to prepare when you resume.

Daily and weekly quests

Quests are another way to earn coins and they count automatically while you play. Open Quests from the home screen and tap Claim once a bar is full.

  • Daily quests: four a day, worth 25, 45, 75 and 110 coins as they get harder. Typical goals are to collect a number of coins, run a set distance in one run, score a target in one run, dodge obstacles, or jump, double jump and slide a number of times. They reset every day at midnight UTC.
  • Weekly quests: three larger goals, worth between 125 and 250 coins each, such as running a total distance, playing 25 runs, or scoring 1,000 in a single run. They reset on Monday.

The shop

Everything in the shop is cosmetic. Nothing you buy makes the game easier, so every player faces the same course. You can mix and match any colour, character, hat and world.

  • Colours: 66, from the free Emerald to the animated rainbow Prism.
  • Characters: 27, including Bot, Slime, Frog, Ninja, Fox, Ghost, Dino, Alien, Snowman, Tiger, Raccoon, Turtle and Pumpkin.
  • Hats: 74, from a simple sprout and beanie to a wizard hat, a unicorn horn, a UFO and a rain cloud.
  • Worlds: 33, including Skyline, Desert, Arctic, Neon, Volcano, Space, Matrix, Crystal Cave, Autumn, Blossom, Jungle, Haunted, Pyramids, Synthwave, Mushroom grove and Abyss. Each world has its own scenery, weather and obstacle colours, and the sky moves through day, dusk and night as you run.

Prices rise with how elaborate an item is. Basic colours and hats are cheap, while animated items and the rarest worlds cost the most, so it is worth collecting a few quest rewards before you decide what to buy first.

Leaderboards, accounts and saving

The leaderboard shows the top 25 runners This week and All time, with a separate board for Classic and for third person. Anyone can view it. To appear on it you need to sign in with Google. You show up as your first name and last initial, and your photo appears only if you have set it to public. You can hide yourself at any time, which also removes your entries.

Playing as a guest is fine and your coins are saved on the game's server, but signing in lets you keep your progress and use it on other devices. You can also add friends, send gifts, and take part in polls and feedback from the home screen. See the privacy page for exactly what is stored.

About the ads

Dash is free and is paid for by ads. An ad may appear between runs, and you can choose to watch one at the game-over screen to double the coins from that run. Watching is always optional. You will never have to watch an ad to play, and a blocked or slow ad never stops the game from running.

Questions and answers

Is Dash free? Yes. There is nothing to buy with real money. Coins are earned by playing.

Do I need an account? No. Guests can play, collect coins and spend them. Sign in if you want your progress on more than one device or a place on the leaderboard.

Does it work on phones? Yes. Dash has touch controls and works in a mobile browser, and it can be added to your home screen as an app.

Why was my score not on the leaderboard? Only signed-in players appear on the boards, and the server checks each run, so scores that the game could not have produced are rejected.

How do I report a bug or suggest something? Use the Polls page in the game to post feedback or a suggestion, or get in touch through gabriellongshaw.co.uk.

Can I see the source code? Yes, Dash is open source on GitHub.
